# W-004 — Evidence verbs encode claim strength and should not be varied decoratively

**Status:** ADOPTED  
**Category:** Claim Strength / Vocabulary

## Original comment

Different verbs such as `suggest`, `indicate`, `show`, and `demonstrate` were being interchanged for style rather than evidence strength.

## Trigger example

A result supported by one dataset is upgraded from `suggests` to `demonstrates` during polishing.

## Underlying concern

Epistemic force changes when evidence verbs change. Decorative synonym rotation can silently overstate the science.

## External verification

### Official guidance

No journal rule assigns a universal numeric threshold to these verbs.

### Recent-paper evidence

The rule is semantic rather than venue-specific: stronger verbs should correspond to stronger and more direct support. Exact boundaries remain contextual.

## Assessment

**Judgment:** Strongly supported

**Reason:** The verbs encode different levels of evidential commitment and therefore should be chosen for meaning, not lexical variety.

## Current rule

Use evidence verbs deliberately:

`is consistent with` / `suggests` → `indicates` → `shows` → `demonstrates` → `establishes`.

Do not upgrade the verb unless the evidence becomes correspondingly stronger. When uncertain, choose the weaker defensible form and state the tested scope.

## Examples

### Avoid

> The experiment demonstrates that the method generalizes across acquisition systems.

when only one acquisition system was tested.

### Prefer

> The experiment shows improved performance on the tested acquisition setting.
