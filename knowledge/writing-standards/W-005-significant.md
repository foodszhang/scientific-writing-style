# W-005 — Reserve `significant` for statistical meaning in quantitative contexts

**Status:** ADOPTED  
**Category:** Claim Strength / Statistical Reporting

## Original comment

Quantitative prose sometimes used `significant` to mean merely "noticeable" or "larger."

## Trigger example

> Method A achieved a significant improvement in Dice.

with no inferential test or interval supporting statistical significance.

## Underlying concern

In quantitative scientific writing, `significant` is readily interpreted as statistically significant. Using it for descriptive magnitude creates ambiguity or an unsupported inferential claim.

## External verification

### Official guidance

No manuscript-wide ban is needed. Statistical terminology should match the actual analysis performed.

### Recent-paper evidence

Formal venue sampling is pending; the semantic risk is sufficiently clear for current adoption.

## Assessment

**Judgment:** Strongly supported

## Current rule

In quantitative contexts, use `significant` only when statistical significance is intended and supported by the stated analysis. Otherwise report the descriptive relation directly, such as `higher`, `lower`, `numerically improved`, or `substantially larger` when the magnitude is explicitly supported.

## Examples

### Avoid

> The method significantly improved Dice.

### Prefer

> The method increased Dice from 0.61 to 0.65.

or, when inferential evidence exists:

> The improvement was statistically significant under the prespecified paired test.
