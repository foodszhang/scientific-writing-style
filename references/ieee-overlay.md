# IEEE Overlay

Apply this file only when the target venue follows IEEE Transactions/Journals/Letters style. Current journal instructions and templates take precedence.

## Editing principle

Treat house-style editing as a layer **after** technical meaning is stable. Do not make unnecessary stylistic changes that risk changing scientific meaning.

## Spelling

Use American English for IEEE manuscripts unless the target publication explicitly says otherwise.

Examples of common normalization:

- `behaviour` → `behavior`
- `centre` → `center`
- `polarisation` → `polarization`

Do not modify proper names, quoted material, code, dataset names, or identifiers.

## Acronyms

- Define an acronym at first use in the abstract and again at first use in the main body when required by the venue.
- Write the term first, followed by the acronym in parentheses.
- Do not add apostrophes to ordinary acronym plurals.
- Avoid unnecessary acronyms used only once or twice.

## Abstract

IEEE's general author style manual specifies a one-paragraph abstract and gives a general 150–250 word range. A specific journal's author instructions override this range.

Keep the abstract self-contained. Avoid citations unless the target venue explicitly permits them.

## Headings

Follow the journal template for heading levels and capitalization. Do not invent heading styles manually in LaTeX when the class file handles them.

## Figures and tables

- Use `Fig.` in IEEE-style figure references where the journal/template requires it.
- Keep figure and table callouts consistent with the template.
- Let the LaTeX class control most caption typography and numbering.
- Do not rewrite a caption merely to mimic house typography if the template already formats it.

## Numbers and units

Follow SI and IEEE conventions supplied by the target template/manual. Keep number-unit formatting consistent and do not silently alter measured values.

## Equations

- Treat displayed equations as part of sentence grammar when appropriate.
- Define symbols before or immediately after first meaningful use.
- Do not duplicate an equation by narrating every symbol if the notation is already clear.
- Preserve equation labels and cross-references during prose revision.

## Contractions and tone

IEEE Transactions prose is formal technical writing. Avoid conversational contractions unless the specific publication allows them and they improve clarity.

## Venue-specific override

For TMI, JBHI, T-BME, TIP, TCI, and other IEEE journals, always check the current journal page/template for constraints beyond the general IEEE manual, including abstract length, graphical abstract requirements, structured sections, and supplementary-material rules.
